Why Movies Break Standard ASR

Cinematic audio is adversarial by design -- every element competes with dialogue

Acoustic

Cocktail Party Problem

Multiple actors speaking simultaneously. Standard single-channel ASR cannot isolate individual voices without additional cues.

Signal

Extreme SNR Variance

Dialogue SNR in movies can drop to -10 dB during action sequences. Background scores and foley effects often dominate the audio mix.

Temporal

Frame-Level Alignment

Subtitles must sync to video within ~40ms. Word-level timestamps from ASR need refinement against scene cuts and shot boundaries.

Linguistic

Expressive & Varied Speech

Whispers, screaming, accents, code-switching, and invented words (fantasy/sci-fi) fall far outside standard ASR training distributions.

Key Insight: Audio-only models hit a fundamental ceiling in these conditions. The visual modality (lip movements, facial expressions) provides a complementary, noise-invariant signal that can resolve ambiguities where audio alone fails.

System Architecture

End-to-end pipeline from raw movie to timed subtitles

Pipeline Overview

Six-stage processing chain from raw movie input to final SRT output

Stage 1

Ingestion & Demuxing

  • FFmpeg demux: separate audio and video tracks
  • Audio: resample to 16kHz mono PCM
  • Video: extract frames at native FPS
  • Scene boundary detection (PySceneDetect)
→
Stage 2

Audio Pre-processing

  • Source separation (Demucs/HTDemucs): vocals vs. music vs. SFX
  • VAD: Voice Activity Detection (Silero VAD)
  • Chunking into utterance-level segments
→
Stage 3

Visual Pre-processing

  • Face detection & tracking (RetinaFace / MTCNN)
  • Mouth ROI cropping (96x96 grayscale)
  • Lip landmark extraction (MediaPipe/dlib)
  • Active Speaker Detection (TalkNet)
→
Stage 4

Feature Extraction

  • Audio: log Mel-spectrogram (80-dim) or raw waveform encoder
  • Visual: ResNet-18 + 3D-Conv temporal encoder on lip crops
  • Positional encoding + modality-specific projections
→
Stage 5

AV Fusion & Decoding

  • Cross-modal attention (audio queries, visual keys)
  • Joint Conformer/Transformer encoder
  • CTC + Attention hybrid decoder
  • Beam search with language model rescoring
→
Stage 6

Post-processing

  • Forced alignment (wav2vec2-based) for word timestamps
  • Speaker diarization labels
  • Subtitle segmentation (line length, reading speed)
  • SRT/VTT generation

Processing Deep Dive

How each modality is processed before fusion

Audio Stream Processing

Raw Waveform → Source Separation (Demucs) → Isolated Vocals
Vocals → 80-dim Log Mel Spectrogram → Audio Encoder (Conformer)

Conformer blocks interleave self-attention with depthwise convolutions, capturing both global context and local acoustic patterns. Output: sequence of audio embeddings Ha ∈ RT×d.

Visual Stream Processing

Video Frames → Face Detect (RetinaFace) → Mouth ROI Crop
96x96 Lip Crops → ResNet-18 + 3D Conv → Visual Encoder (Transformer)

3D convolutions capture temporal lip dynamics across frames. The visual encoder outputs embeddings Hv ∈ RT'×d aligned to the audio timeline via interpolation.

Cross-Modal Fusion

Ha (Audio) Hv (Visual) → Cross-Attention → Joint Repr.

Cross-modal attention lets the audio stream "attend" to relevant visual frames. When audio is noisy, the attention weights shift toward the visual modality. When the speaker is off-screen, audio takes precedence.

Q = WqHa,   K = WkHv,   V = WvHv
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKT / √d) V

Decoding & Alignment

Fused Features → CTC + Attn Decoder → Token Sequence

Hybrid CTC/Attention decoding: CTC provides monotonic alignment constraint, attention decoder captures linguistic dependencies. Beam search with LM shallow fusion for final output.

Forced alignment (wav2vec2-align) then maps decoded tokens back to precise timestamps for subtitle cue points.

Audio-Visual ASR Architecture

How AV-HuBERT and Auto-AVSR fuse lip reading with speech recognition

AV-HuBERT (2023)

Self-Supervised Audio-Visual Pre-training

  • Masks segments of both audio and video input simultaneously
  • Predicts cluster assignments from a joint AV representation
  • Audio encoder: modified Transformer on log mel-filterbanks
  • Visual encoder: ResNet-18 on mouth ROI crops + temporal positional encoding
  • Early fusion via concatenation of modality embeddings before the shared Transformer
  • Fine-tuned with CTC loss on labeled speech data (LRS3, VoxCeleb2)
Why this matters for movies: Because the model learns joint audio-visual representations during pre-training, it naturally shifts reliance to the visual stream when audio degrades (music, explosions).
Auto-AVSR (2024)

End-to-End Production Pipeline

  • Automatic preprocessing: face detection, tracking, and mouth ROI extraction built into the pipeline
  • Visual frontend: ResNet-18 + 3D convolution + Temporal Convolutional Network
  • Audio frontend: log filterbank features + Conformer encoder
  • Late fusion via cross-attention layers in the decoder
  • Hybrid CTC/Attention decoder with LM shallow fusion
  • Achieves SOTA WER on LRS2 (1.0%) and LRS3 (0.9%) benchmarks
Key advantage: Unlike AV-HuBERT which needs pre-cropped lip videos, Auto-AVSR handles raw video input end-to-end, making it practical for movie subtitling.

Proposed Experiments

Systematic evaluation of audio-only vs. audio-visual approaches

Experiment Design

Six experiments to characterize when and why multimodal fusion improves subtitle quality

1

Noise Robustness Curve

Compare Whisper vs. AV-HuBERT vs. Auto-AVSR at SNR levels from clean (40dB) down to -10dB using additive movie soundtracks.

Metric: WER at each SNR level
Dataset: LRS3 test set + movie-score noise injection
Hypothesis: AV models degrade gracefully below 0dB where audio-only collapses
2

Visual Modality Ablation

Systematically degrade the visual stream: lower resolution lip crops, occluded faces, off-screen speakers. Measure WER impact.

Metric: WER delta vs. full visual input
Conditions: 96px, 48px, 24px ROI; 25%, 50%, 75% occlusion
Goal: Understand the model's fallback behavior when visual cues are degraded
3

Source Separation Impact

Run the ASR pipeline with and without a Demucs source-separation preprocessing stage. Compare on dialogue-heavy vs. action scenes.

Metric: WER, SI-SDR of separated vocals
Dataset: Curated movie clips (10 dialogue, 10 action)
Goal: Quantify whether source separation helps AV models or is redundant
4

Overlapping Speaker Resolution

Two speakers talking simultaneously. Compare audio-only diarization (pyannote) vs. AV diarization using active speaker detection (TalkNet).

Metric: DER (Diarization Error Rate), WER per speaker
Dataset: AMI meeting corpus + synthetic movie overlaps
Goal: Measure visual contribution to the cocktail party problem
5

Domain Adaptation

Fine-tune AV-HuBERT (pre-trained on LRS3) on a small labeled movie dataset. Measure WER improvement and genre transfer (drama vs. action).

Metric: WER before/after fine-tuning
Data: ~50 hrs movie clips with ground-truth subtitles
Goal: Assess data efficiency of AV pre-training for domain shift
6

Non-Speech Sound Captioning

Use Qwen-Audio to detect and label non-speech events ([gunshot], [door creaks], [ominous music]) alongside dialogue transcription.

Metric: F1 for sound event detection; subtitle readability score
Baseline: Manual SDH (Subtitles for Deaf/HoH) annotations
Goal: Move beyond transcription toward true closed captioning

Primary Metrics

  • WER -- Word Error Rate (substitutions + insertions + deletions / total words)
  • CER -- Character Error Rate (finer granularity for accented speech)
  • DER -- Diarization Error Rate (speaker attribution accuracy)

Subtitle-Specific Metrics

  • Timestamp Accuracy -- Mean absolute error of subtitle cue points vs. ground truth (ms)
  • Reading Speed -- Characters per second; must stay within 15-25 CPS for readability
  • BLEU / METEOR -- Translation quality (for multilingual subtitle generation)
  • SED F1 -- Sound Event Detection F1 (for non-speech captioning)
